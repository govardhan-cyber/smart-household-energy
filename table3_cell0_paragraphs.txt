Paragraph 12 (Style: Normal): '1. INTRODUCTION'
Paragraph 13 (Style: Normal): '1.1 Problem Statement'
Paragraph 14 (Style: Normal): 'In modern residential sectors, electrical energy costs represent a significant portion of household monthly expenses. However, domestic consumers face a severe asymmetry of information. Standard utility bills only present aggregated raw numbers (e.g., total energy consumed in kWh and total amount due) without dissecting where and how this power is utilized. Consumers lack clarity on slab-wise tariff charges, fixed meter fees, and taxes added by State Distribution Companies (DISCOMs). Additionally, manual calculation of daily appliance loads and evaluating the feasibility of transitioning to renewable energy resources (such as solar rooftop panels) requires complex computations and mathematical formulas that the average homeowner cannot calculate easily. Existing energy monitoring systems are either expensive IoT-based hardware devices or static utility portals that lack custom tariff modeling, personalized scheduling advice, and responsive user interfaces.'
Paragraph 15 (Style: Normal): '1.2 Project Objective'
Paragraph 16 (Style: Normal): 'The primary objective of the Smart Household Energy Manager project is to develop a highly interactive, accessible, and premium web-based dashboard application. This software empowers users to upload utility bills for automated analysis, perform granular audit logs of individual appliance consumptions, simulate rooftop solar ROI metrics, and ask an integrated AI chatbot questions about energy conservation. Crucially, the system features a custom DISCOM Tariff editor, letting users adjust slab definitions in real-time to align with regional electricity board rates. The user interface prioritizes high-fidelity visual aesthetics (such as glassmorphism, responsive data grids, and smooth state animations) to lower the cognitive barrier and encourage user engagement.'

Paragraph 155 (Style: Normal): '8. CHALLENGES FACED'
Paragraph 156 (Style: Normal): 'Several technical challenges were addressed during the engineering process:'
Paragraph 157 (Style: List Bullet): 'State Syncing and Re-renders: Storing tariff parameters in the root context meant that updating a slab rule could trigger cascade re-renders across the dashboard, causing input lag. This was resolved by using debounced state updates in the tariff editor.'
Paragraph 158 (Style: List Bullet): 'Responsive Table Design: Traditional HTML tables presenting solar system capacities overflowed on mobile devices. We refactored these into flex-grid cards that stack vertically on mobile and stretch horizontally on wide monitors.'
Paragraph 159 (Style: List Bullet): 'CSS Color Configuration: Encountered a design bug where settings wrapper containers would not adjust correctly during dark mode toggles. Ripgrep searches isolated typo classes (`slate-955` instead of `slate-950`). We resolved this by explicitly mapping custom color bounds in the Tailwind configuration file.'
Paragraph 160 (Style: List Bullet): 'TypeScript Interface Safety: Managing dynamic slab objects (where elements are added or deleted from lists dynamically) required strict TypeScript types to prevent compilation crashes during database transfers.'
